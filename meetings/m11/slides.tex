\begin{frame}{The Number That Decides It}

The assistant costs \textyen 2{,}500 per store-week. Gross margin on incremental sales is \textbf{25\%}.

\bigskip

\[
0.25 \times \Delta\text{sales} \;>\; 2{,}500 .
\]

\bigskip

\[
\Delta^{*} \;=\; \frac{2{,}500}{0.25} \;=\; \textbf{\textyen 10{,}000 per store-week}
\]
\end{alertblock}

\bigskip

One of the analyst's numbers is below it and one is above. Every estimate today is a weekly sales lift. The only question is \textbf{which side of \textyen 10{,}000} it lands on --- and for \emph{which} stores, \emph{how long} after adoption.
\end{frame}

\section{Identification: Contamination}
% ====================================================================

\begin{frame}{Why the Already-Treated Control Is Contaminated}

Store E's effect is $\tau_E(0) = 8$ in its first treated week and $\tau_E(1) = 16$ in its second. Between weeks 2 and 3, E moves by the common trend \emph{plus} the growth in its own effect:
\[
\Delta Y_E = 4 + \bigl[\tau_E(1) - \tau_E(0)\bigr] = 4 + 8 = 12 .
\]
\pause
Using E as the control for L subtracts all of that:
\[
\text{DiD}_4 = \underbrace{\bigl[4 + \tau_L(0)\bigr]}_{\Delta Y_L} - \underbrace{\bigl[4 + \tau_E(1) - \tau_E(0)\bigr]}_{\Delta Y_E}
= \tau_L(0) - \bigl[\tau_E(1) - \tau_E(0)\bigr] = 8 - 8 = 0 .
\]

\begin{alertblock}{The pattern}
An already-treated control removes the \textbf{change in its own effect}. Growing effects bias the comparison \emph{down}; fading effects, \emph{up}. Only a flat effect makes it harmless.
\end{alertblock}

\small Parallel trends was never violated: the trend is exactly $+4$ everywhere. The failure is in what was called a control.
\end{frame}

% ====================================================================
\begin{frame}{What Two-Way Fixed Effects Does With All Four}

Run $Y_{it} = \alpha_i + \lambda_t + \beta D_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}$ on the nine cells. The coefficient is a \textbf{weighted average of the four $2 \times 2$ comparisons}, with weights set by group sizes and by how long each group spends treated (Goodman-Bacon 2021):

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llrr}
\toprule
& \textbf{Comparison} & \textbf{Weight} & \textbf{DiD} \\
\midrule
& E vs N, all weeks & $1/3$ & $12$ \\
& L vs N, all weeks & $1/3$ & $8$ \\
& E vs L, L not yet treated & $1/6$ & $8$ \\
& L vs E, E already treated & $1/6$ & $\textcolor{warnred}{0}$ \\
\midrule
& $\widehat\beta_{\text{TWFE}}$ & & $\tfrac{1}{3}(12) + \tfrac{1}{3}(8) + \tfrac{1}{6}(8) + \tfrac{1}{6}(0) = \mathbf{8}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\small The first row's DiD is 12: it averages E's two treated weeks ($8$ and $16$) against week 1.

\medskip
\pause

The true average over the three treated store-weeks is $(8 + 16 + 8)/3 = \mathbf{10.7}$. TWFE returns $\mathbf{8}$, one-sixth of its weight spent on a comparison that measures nothing.

\medskip
\begin{center}
Against a break-even of $10$: \textcolor{warnred}{TWFE says no}; the truth says \textcolor{sbgreen}{yes, once the effect has grown}.
\end{center}
\end{frame}

% ====================================================================
\begin{frame}{This Is Not a Small-Sample Curiosity}

Last time's TWFE, with a single adoption date, \emph{was} the $2 \times 2$ DiD. Nothing was wrong with the estimator; the panel had only clean comparisons to offer.

\bigskip

With staggered adoption the same regression silently adds the contaminated ones. Its weight on them grows with

\begin{itemize}
    \item the \textbf{spread} of adoption dates,
    \item the share of the panel that is \emph{already treated} when later cohorts adopt,
    \item and it bites hardest when effects \textbf{change with event time} --- which is exactly when Dana's question (``how long until it pays?'') is interesting.
\end{itemize}

\bigskip
\pause

\begin{center}
\textbf{So: estimate the effect one cohort and one period at a time, using only clean controls, and aggregate afterwards.}
\end{center}
\end{frame}

\section{Identification and Estimation: Group-Time Effects}
% ====================================================================

\begin{frame}[shrink=6]{The Group-Time ATT}

\begin{block}{Definition (Callaway and Sant'Anna 2021)}
For a cohort that adopted at $g$, and a calendar period $t \geq g$,
\[
\text{ATT}(g, t) \;=\; \E\bigl[Y_t(g) - Y_t(\infty) \,\big|\, G = g\bigr] .
\]
\end{block}

One number per cohort per period. Nothing is averaged yet.
\pause
\begin{block}{Identification}
Under parallel trends between cohort $g$ and the never-treated (or the not-yet-treated) from a base week $b < g$ to $t$ --- the last week \emph{before any anticipation}, $g - 1$ in general, $g - 3$ here (next slide) ---
\[
\text{ATT}(g, t) = \bigl\{\E[Y_t \mid G = g] - \E[Y_{b} \mid G = g]\bigr\} - \bigl\{\E[Y_t \mid G = \infty] - \E[Y_{b} \mid G = \infty]\bigr\} .
\]
\end{block}

Each is a $2 \times 2$ DiD with a \textbf{clean} control and a base period just before adoption.

\smallskip
\textbf{On the three stores:} $\text{ATT}(E, 2) = 8$, $\text{ATT}(E, 3) = 16$, $\text{ATT}(L, 3) = 8$ --- comparisons 1--3. The fourth never enters.
\end{frame}

% ====================================================================

For each target, which stores may stand in for $Y_{it}(\infty)$? Decide it from the calendar, before any outcome.

\section{Estimation: The Flagship}

% [RELEASE R4 — the flagship's series and the donor pool; keep separate from R3]
\begin{frame}{One Store, Two Years Ahead}

The flagship --- the vendor's co-development store, with an engineer on site and a custom-tuned assistant: a different version of the treatment --- went live in week 20, thirty-three weeks before anyone else, and has \NUM{84}{case04-staffing: flagship post-adoption weeks} post-adoption weeks.

\smallskip

Its before/after difference is $+$\textyen \NUM{18{,}700}{sim staffing-panel v1: flagship raw before/after mean difference} per week. Dana's question: \emph{how much of that is the assistant?}

\smallskip
\pause

A $2 \times 2$ needs a control. Which store?

\begin{itemize}
    \item The chain's other large-format store? Different city.
    \item The average of all never-treated stores? A different format, a different trend.
    \item \textbf{A weighted average of never-treated stores}, weighted so that it tracked the flagship \emph{before} adoption?
\end{itemize}

\smallskip

The third is \textbf{synthetic control}: the M10 idea --- borrow the trend --- with the control group chosen by the data.

\end{frame}

% ====================================================================
\begin{frame}[shrink=9]{Synthetic Control}

Treated unit $1$, adopts at $T_0$. Donor pool $j = 2, \dots, J{+}1$ of never-treated units.

\begin{block}{The estimator (Abadie, Diamond and Hainmueller 2010)}
Choose weights $w_j \geq 0$, $\sum_j w_j = 1$, to minimise the pre-period gap
\[
\sum_{t \leq T_0} \Bigl( Y_{1t} - \sum_j w_j Y_{jt} \Bigr)^{2},
\]
then estimate the effect in each post-period as $\;\widehat\tau_t = Y_{1t} - \sum_j w_j\, Y_{jt}$.
\end{block}

\pause
Three things the constraints buy:
\begin{itemize}\small
    \item \textbf{Non-negative, summing to one:} the synthetic store lives inside the donors' range. No extrapolation.
    \item \textbf{Sparse:} most weights come out zero. You can name the stores the comparison rests on.
    \item \textbf{Pre-period fit is the credential.} A synthetic store that did not track the real one before adoption has no claim to track it after.
\end{itemize}

\scriptsize \textbf{Design first:} fix the donor pool, the fitting weeks and the criterion before looking at week 20 onwards. Then test the frozen weights on held-out pre-weeks: fit on weeks 1--14, check 15--19.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What One Store Can and Cannot Tell You}

\textbf{No standard error.} One treated unit; the ``sample'' is a single series. The interval you are used to does not exist here. What exists instead:

\begin{itemize}\small
    \item \textbf{In-place placebos.} Treat each donor as if adopted at week 20, fit its synthetic control, and compare post/pre gap ratios. If the flagship's is not among the most extreme, the gap is what the method produces by chance. On the real panel the flagship ranks \NUM{1}{sim v1: rank of flagship post/pre RMSPE ratio among 60 in-place placebos} of 60 --- a \emph{descriptive} ranking: it would be a $p$-value of $1/60$ only if the flagship had been as likely as any donor to go first.
    \item \textbf{In-time placebos.} Move the adoption date earlier, refit, and check that no ``effect'' appears before week 20.
    \item \textbf{A local-boom check.} The same comparison on never-treated stores in the flagship's own city, around week 20.
\end{itemize}

\pause
\begin{alertblock}{And two things no placebo fixes}
The flagship was chosen \emph{first}, almost certainly because it was expected to do well. And a shock that hit its format harder than the donors' would look exactly like an effect.
\end{alertblock}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Key Takeaways}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \spinestep{Estimand.} $\text{ATT}(g, t)$ and its event-time path: a rollout decision needs the path and its break-even crossing, not one average.
    \item \spinestep{Identification.} An already-treated control subtracts the change in its own effect. Choose \emph{eligible} controls and base weeks from the calendar before computing anything. Parallel trends is still an assumption; randomising the order would make it design.
    \item \spinestep{Estimation.} TWFE averages every $2\times2$ the panel offers, contaminated ones included. Estimate cohort-by-period effects with clean controls, then aggregate on purpose.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic control is DiD with a data-chosen control group.} Freeze the donor design; its credential is the pre-period fit; its placebo rank is descriptive; it has no standard error.
    \item \textbf{Keep flagship and cohort evidence distinct.} One bounds the upside; the other forecasts.
\end{itemize}
